# PARI reply: use of three article photographs in the study

PARI (Zahra, People's Archive of Rural India) sent three replies to `pari-image-permission-request.md`, all reproduced verbatim below and forwarded by the project lead:

- the second restates the permission and asks for the applicable licence information;
- the third adds photo 07, confirms the exact credit line, and offers the original high-resolution files.

**Status: permission granted** for photos 04, 06 and 07 by Rahul Maganti, on these conditions, which ingest must follow:

- **Where:** both study conditions and the public demonstration only.
- **Form:** uncropped and unaltered, at most 768 pixels on the longest side.
- **Text:** PARI's own photo caption, plus exactly this credit, as confirmed in the third reply: "Photo: Rahul Maganti / People's Archive of Rural India (PARI). CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, used with permission." The licence name links to the licence.
- **Duration:** up to five years from first publication. Set `licenceExpiresOn` at ingest; the parity check refuses an expired licence.

PARI has offered the original high-resolution files of photos 04, 06 and 07, which have not been received yet. Before ingest, the files must still be checked:

- that each matches the published frame, with no crop or edit;
- that each is at least 1200 px on the long edge (photo 07 is 824 × 1120 as published);
- its SHA-256 recorded.

Still unanswered: whether the identifiable artisans in photo 06 consented to publication.

This does not yet add any photograph to the study. Under the DR-020 balance rule, a kind of figure is shown only when all three traditions have one:

- photo 06 (kind B, the printing table) also needs an Ajrakh printing-table tool concept;
- photo 04 (kind C, the dye bath) has no Sanganeri counterpart;
- photo 07 (an empty workshed on a rainy day, for the weather-dependent scheduling concept) has no Ajrakh or Sanganeri counterpart.

See the process-photo proposal.

Dear Saurav, Ankit and Priyadarshi,

Thank you for reaching out to PARI and for sharing the details of your research study at IIT Kharagpur. Your work comparing different ways of presenting knowledge about Indian hand-block-printed textiles sounds interesting, and we are happy to support your non-commercial academic research.

We are pleased to grant permission for the use of Photo 04 and Photo 06 from Rahul Maganti’s article, *“A muted maggam, a bleeding block print”*, for the purposes described in your email, subject to the following conditions:

- The photographs may be used in the conventional web page and virtual museum developed for your study, including the public demonstration described in your request.
- The images should remain uncropped and unaltered and should be reproduced at a maximum of 768 pixels on the longest side.
- Please include the photo captions and credit each image as follows: **Photo: Rahul Maganti / People's Archive of Rural India (PARI)**.
- The photographs may be used for up to five years from the date of first publication for the stated research purposes. Any use beyond this scope would require further permission.

We appreciate your care in clarifying the licensing status before using the photographs. Regarding the CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 licence, please follow the applicable licence terms wherever they apply; this email confirms permission for the specific use described above.

We will also check on the availability of the original, higher-resolution files for Photos 04, 06 and 07 and get back to you regarding their sharing.

Thank you again for your interest in PARI’s work and for ensuring that the photographs are properly credited. We wish you and your team the very best with your research.

Warm regards,
Zahra
People's Archive of Rural India (PARI)
